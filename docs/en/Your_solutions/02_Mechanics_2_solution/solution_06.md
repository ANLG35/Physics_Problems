# Physics Problem: Energy Loss in a Bouncing Ball

## Problem Statement
A tennis ball is dropped from a height of $2.0\text{ m}$. After each bounce, it loses $30\%$ of its mechanical energy. To what height does it rise after the second bounce?

## Solution

### 1. Initial Potential Energy ($E_0$)
The energy at the start is purely potential energy:
$$E_0 = mgh_0$$
Where $h_0 = 2.0\text{ m}$.

### 2. Energy after the First Bounce ($E_1$)
If the ball loses $30\%$ of its energy, it retains $70\%$ ($0.70$) of its energy:
$$E_1 = 0.70 \times E_0$$
Since $E \propto h$ (because $E = mgh$), the height is directly proportional to the energy:
$$h_1 = 0.70 \times h_0$$
$$h_1 = 0.70 \times 2.0 = 1.4\text{ m}$$

### 3. Energy after the Second Bounce ($E_2$)
The ball loses another $30\%$ of its remaining energy during the second bounce:
$$E_2 = 0.70 \times E_1$$
Again, substituting height for energy:
$$h_2 = 0.70 \times h_1$$
$$h_2 = 0.70 \times 1.4$$

**$h_2 = 0.98\text{ meters}$**

### Summary Table
| Event | Energy Remaining (%) | Height (m) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Initial Drop | $100\%$ | $2.00$ |
| 1st Bounce | $70\%$ | $1.40$ |
| 2nd Bounce | $49\%$ | $0.98$ |
