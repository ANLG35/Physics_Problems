# Physics Problem: Conservation of Energy in a Pendulum

## Problem Statement
A pendulum with a length of $1.0\text{ meter}$ is released from an initial angle of $15^\circ$. What is the speed of the pendulum bob at the bottom of its swing?

## Solution using Conservation of Energy
To find the speed at the bottom, we use the principle of conservation of mechanical energy:
$$E_{initial} = E_{final}$$
$$mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

### 1. Finding the Height ($h$)
The vertical height $h$ relative to the lowest point is calculated using the length $L$ and the release angle $\theta$:
$$h = L - L\cos(\theta) = L(1 - \cos\theta)$$

Given:
* $L = 1.0\text{ m}$
* $\theta = 15^\circ$

$$h = 1.0 \times (1 - \cos(15^\circ))$$
$$h = 1.0 \times (1 - 0.9659) \approx 0.0341\text{ m}$$

### 2. Calculating the Velocity ($v$)
Now, we solve for $v$ in the energy equation (mass $m$ cancels out):
$$v = \sqrt{2gh}$$
$$v = \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 0.0341}$$
$$v = \sqrt{0.669}$$
**$v \approx 0.818\text{ m/s}$**
