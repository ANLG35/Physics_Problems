# Physics Problem: Energy Conservation and Inelastic Collision

## Problem Statement
A $0.5\text{ kg}$ block slides down a frictionless track from a height of $3.0\text{ m}$. At the bottom, it collides and sticks to a $1.5\text{ kg}$ block, which is initially at rest. What is the speed of the combined mass just after the collision?

## Solution

### 1. Speed of the first block at the bottom ($v_1$)
Using the conservation of energy, the potential energy at the top converts to kinetic energy at the bottom:
$$m_1gh = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2$$
$$v_1 = \sqrt{2gh}$$

Given:
* $m_1 = 0.5\text{ kg}$
* $h = 3.0\text{ m}$
* $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$

$$v_1 = \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 3.0} = \sqrt{58.86} \approx 7.67\text{ m/s}$$

### 2. Speed after the inelastic collision ($v_f$)
Since the blocks stick together, this is a perfectly inelastic collision. We use the conservation of momentum:
$$m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = (m_1 + m_2)v_f$$

Given:
* $m_2 = 1.5\text{ kg}$
* $v_2 = 0\text{ m/s}$ (at rest)

$$(0.5 \times 7.67) + (1.5 \times 0) = (0.5 + 1.5)v_f$$
$$3.835 = 2.0 \times v_f$$
$$v_f = \frac{3.835}{2.0}$$

**$v_f \approx 1.92\text{ m/s}$**
